\section*{Notes to the table}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=14pt]
\item[$a$] The Brio--Wu deck sets $(C_1,C_2,C_{\min})=\big(((N+1)/N)^2,\ 0.5\,(N+1)/N,\ 0.06\,(N+1)/N\big)$ on the kernel's
$h_e/(N+1)$, i.e.\ $(1,0.5,0.06)$ on Dao \& Nazarov's $\Delta_e=h_e/N$, the same at every $N$ for a fixed number of DOFs. The paper
varies $C_{\min}$ (\code{JEXPRESSO\_BW\_CMIN}).
\item[$b$] The Euler vortex uses the CompEuler kernel: $\mu_{\rm res}|_e=C_1\Delta_e^2\,n_\rho\,r_e$ and
$\mu_{\max}|_e=C_2\Delta_e\lVert\rho\rVert_{\infty,\Omega_e}\lVert\lVert\vv\rVert+c\rVert_{\infty,\Omega_e}$ are dynamic ($n_\rho$: the
density denominator); the momentum residual enters as one vector norm; heat conduction $\mathrm{P}\mu/(\gamma-1)$ with the artificial
Prandtl number $\mathrm{P}=$ \code{:Pr} $=0.1$ (default). The deck's \code{:dsgs\_Prt => 0.7} is not read by this kernel.
\item[$c$] The paper's $160\times70$ run is \code{fluxEmergenceSon2025DSGS}. Its sibling \code{fluxEmergenceSon2025} ($80\times35$,
$\Delta t=7.5\cdot10^{-3}$, $t_{\rm end}=54$, a case positivity floor as stage limiter) diffuses $\qq-\qq_e$ with $\nu$ (no
$\rho_e$ weight) and is otherwise the same.
\item[$d$] The deck's default is one point of the convergence figure; \code{JEXPRESSO\_SV\_NOP}, \code{\_NELX} (\code{JEXPRESSO\_EV\_*}
for the Euler vortex) set the others, with $\Delta t$ from the rule.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Where the paper's text and the decks differ}
These are the red entries of the table, with what each one changes.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Sensor, (11).} The paper's (11) reads the assembled rate $M^{-1}\mathcal{R}$, which is the legacy sensor (L) when
it is evaluated at every stage. The smooth vortices, Brio--Wu and the jet run (R): the element's own rate $M_e^{-1}\mathcal{R}_e$,
once per step. Orszag--Tang, the rotor, the blast and the flux emergence run (L).
\item \textbf{Mass diffusion.} The paper sets $\lambda_1=0$ (mass untouched); every deck has \code{:}$\mu$\code{[1] = 1}, so $\nu\nabla\rho$
is on, in all eight setups.
\item \textbf{Energy.} The paper's Table \emph{split} has $\lambda_4\bar\rho_e\mu|_e\,\gamma/((\gamma-1)\mathrm{Pr}_t)$ on
$\nabla T$ with $\mathrm{Pr}_t=0.7$. No deck uses that combination: the physical-form decks (the MHD vortex, Orszag--Tang) take
Dao \& Nazarov's $\bar\rho_e\nu/\mathrm{Pr}_t$ (\code{:dsgs\_nazarov\_energy}) with $\mathrm{Pr}_t=1$, and the conserved-form decks
diffuse $E$ itself (the flux emergence, its thermal part only).
\item \textbf{Ohmic heating.} The paper's remark says the resistive term is not returned to the energy. The physical form (the MHD
vortex, Orszag--Tang) adds the resistive work $\nabla\cdot(\eta\BB\cdot\nabla\BB)$ to the energy, so the magnetic energy removed
reappears as heat and the total energy is conserved.
\item \textbf{Conserved form.} The paper's stabilized system (stress, $\kappa_e\nabla T$, $\bm\tau\cdot\vv$) is the physical form.
Brio--Wu, the rotor, the blast, the jet and the flux emergence diffuse the conserved variables with one kinematic $\nu$ instead:
no stress, no work terms.
\item \textbf{Normalization, (12) and (16).} (12) normalizes by the domain spread; the rotor, the blast, the jet and the flux
emergence normalize by the element's own spread, floored at the element's scales. (16) has $\erel=10^{-3}$; every deck runs
$\erel=1$, the floor being the full physical scale.
\item \textbf{Constants and length.} $C_{\min}$ is not in (12)--(14); Brio--Wu runs 0.06 and the flux emergence 0.03. Brio--Wu
uses $\Delta_e=h_e/N$, not (15).
\item \textbf{Per stage or per step.} The paper's algorithm evaluates \dsgs{} once per right-hand-side call (every stage); the
(R) decks compute $\mu|_e$ once per step.
\item \textbf{Not in the paper.} The start-up hold (2 steps; none in the blast and the jet), the fast-speed floors (blast, jet),
the residual of $\rho e$ (blast) and the reference weight $\rho_e$ (flux emergence).
\item \textbf{Discretization.} The rotor (paper $256^2$ at $N=4$; deck $128^2$ at $N=3$), the blast (paper $128^2$, $256^2$ at
$N=4$; deck $64^2$ at $N=3$) and Orszag--Tang (paper $128^2$ to $512^2$ in the figures and $32^2$ with $\Delta t=5\cdot10^{-4}$ in
the text; deck $64^2$, $\Delta t=2\cdot10^{-4}$) were run on other meshes than the decks'. The flux-emergence figure is at
$t=51\,\tau_0$; the deck stops at 50.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Defaults that changed while the paper's runs were made}
A run made before these dates used the old behaviour, whatever its deck says now.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{2026-09-10} (\code{39362212}): the residual sensor (R) becomes the default and \code{:dsgs\_sensor => "legacy"}
keeps the old one. Before, every \dsgs{} run used (L).
\item \textbf{2026-09-23} (\code{8a81279c}): the floors (16) go from $\erel=10^{-3}$ to $\erel=1$ (\code{:dsgs\_rel}). Before,
a nearly uniform field (the density of a vortex, $B_x$ in a shock tube) had its residual divided by $10^{-3}$ of its scale.
\item \textbf{2026-10-14} (\code{b956ccd} for \code{DSGS\_MHD}, \code{e12fca8} for the CompEuler \code{DSGS}): with the residual
sensor, $\mu|_e$ is computed once per step. Before, at every stage, where the residual stalls at $O(\Delta t)$ on smooth flow
(\code{dsgsCutoff.pdf}).
\item \textbf{Last change of each deck:} MHD vortex and Brio--Wu \code{b956ccd} (2026-10-14), Euler vortex \code{e12fca8}
(2026-10-14), Orszag--Tang \code{6829a2c} (10-08), rotor \code{7bd7bc2} (10-09), blast \code{e4fcf14} (10-12), jet
\code{2590be6} (10-12), flux emergence \code{c1d5b46} (10-13).
\end{itemize}
