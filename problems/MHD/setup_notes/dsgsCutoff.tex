{\Large\bfseries DynSGS without and with the smoothness cutoff $c=10^{-3}$}\\[2pt]
{\small In the notation of the paper (\emph{The shock capturing model}): equation numbers (11)--(16) and Remark~1 are the
paper's, (C), (L), (R) this note's. Measured on the smooth Alfv\'en wave (\code{problems/MHD/alfvenWaveBabbarRanocha2026};
Babbar \& Ranocha 2026, \S5.5.1), branch \branch{}, commit \code{e40bc3e}. The cutoff is the deck entry \code{AW\_CUTOFF}
(\code{:dsgs\_cutoff}), $10^{-3}$ by default.}

\vspace{-2pt}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}

\textbf{1.\ The coefficient, and where the cutoff enters.}
With $\Delta_e=h_e/(N+1)$ (15), $C_1=1$, $C_2=\tfrac12$, and $\mu_{\max}|_e$, $\mu|_e$ as in (13)--(14), the code computes
\begin{equation}
\mres=C_1\,\Delta_e^2\,\max\big(0,\ r_e-c\big),\qquad
r_e=\max_{i\in\Ic}\frac{\Linf{R_i}{\Omega_e}}{\max\big(\Linf{q_i-\avg{q_i}}{\Omega},\ \sigma_i\big)} .
\tag{C}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Without the cutoff}, $c=0$, (C) is (12) with the floors (16). \textbf{With it}, $c=10^{-3}$ is subtracted from the
normalized residual $r_e$ first, so $\mu|_e=0$ wherever $r_e\le c$, and $\mu|_e$ stays continuous across the threshold (it is
not an on/off switch). The floors are those of (16) with $\erel=1$ (\code{:dsgs\_rel}; (16) says $10^{-3}$):
$\sigma_\rho=\bar\rho$, $\sigma_{\rho\vv}=\bar\rho\bar c$, $\sigma_E=\bar\rho\bar c^{\,2}$, $\sigma_{\BB}=\sqrt{\bar\rho}\,\bar c$.
For this wave $\bar\rho=1$ and $\bar c=\sqrt{\gamma\bar p/\bar\rho}=0.42$, the sound speed of the mean state ($\bar p=0.107$, the
equation of state evaluated on $\avg{E}$, $\avg{\rho\vv}$, $\avg{\BB}$). The wave's spread ($\le0.1$) is below the floors, so the
momentum and $\BB$ residuals are divided by $\sigma=\bar\rho\bar c=0.42$, and $r_e$ is a rate (1/time).

\textbf{2.\ The two sensors: what $R_i$ is.}
``Legacy'' is the code's name for the sensor of the runs before September 2026; ``residual'' is the default since. Both
evaluate a time difference minus a spatial rate, on the step-cadenced history of Remark~1 ($\qq^n,\qq^{n-1},\qq^{n-2}$, rolled
once per step). Let $\qq^{(s)}$ be the Runge--Kutta stage state at $t^n+\theta_s\Delta t$ ($\theta_1=0$, so $\qq^{(1)}=\qq^n$),
$\Rc_{e,i}$ the bracket of the semi-discrete statement $M\,d\qq_h/dt=\Rc(\qq_h)$ \emph{before} $\bigoplus_e$ (the element's
own right-hand side), and $M_e$ the element's lumped mass. The \emph{legacy} sensor is (11) at every stage, the stage state taking
the place of $\qq^n$; the \emph{residual} sensor is (11) at the first stage only, with the element's own rate in place of the
assembled one:
\begin{align}
\text{legacy:}\quad & R_i\big|_p=\frac{3q_{i,p}^{(s)}-4q_{i,p}^{\,n}+q_{i,p}^{\,n-1}}{2\Delta t}
  -\big(M^{-1}\Rc_i(\qq^{(s)})\big)_p\qquad\text{at every stage } s, \tag{L}\\[2pt]
\text{residual:}\quad & R_i\big|_p=\frac{3q_{i,p}^{\,n}-4q_{i,p}^{\,n-1}+q_{i,p}^{\,n-2}}{2\Delta t}
  -\big(M_e^{-1}\Rc_{e,i}(\qq^{n})\big)_p\qquad\text{at } s=1;\ \mu|_e \text{ held for the step.} \tag{R}
\end{align}

\textbf{What (L) measures.} Since $M\,d\qq_h/dt=\Rc$, $\big(M^{-1}\Rc_i(\qq^{(s)})\big)_p$ \emph{is} the rate $\partial_tq_{i,p}$ the
integrator uses at that stage, so (L) has no spatial term left: it is the error of its time difference as an estimate of
$\partial_tq$, a property of the solution's time history and not of its spatial resolution. And that difference is not
$\partial_tq$: its newest point $\qq^{(s)}$ is $\theta_s\Delta t$ after $\qq^n$, not $\Delta t$. Expanding about $t^n$,
$(3q^{(s)}-4q^n+q^{n-1})/(2\Delta t)=\tfrac12(3\theta_s-1)\,\partial_tq+O(\Delta t)$: $-\tfrac12\partial_tq$ at $s=1$, and $\partial_tq$
only at $\theta_s=1$. Hence $R_i\approx\tfrac32(\theta_s-1)\,\partial_tq_i$, the rate at which the solution changes
($\lambda\,\partial_xq$ for a wave), on resolved and unresolved flow alike: a gradient sensor rather than a residual. On this wave
$r_e\approx\tfrac32\,\omega A/\sigma\approx2$ at $s=1$ ($\omega=2\pi$, $A=0.1$), so $\mres\approx2\Delta_e^2$, $O(h^2)$ whatever $N$.
$N=4$ on $16^2$ ends with a state error of $3.5\cdot10^{-3}$, against $1.9\cdot10^{-6}$ for (R) without the cutoff,
$4.2\cdot10^{-7}$ with it, and $4.3\cdot10^{-7}$ for Galerkin; no cutoff $c\ll2$ changes that. A consistent time difference would
not rescue the assembled rate either: the residual is then that difference's truncation error alone, as small at a shock as on a
smooth wave (measured on \code{sod1d}: $\mu$ at the shock $0.5\%$ of the cap). (L) sees a shock only through the
inconsistency of its stencil.

\textbf{What (R) measures.} With the LGL rule (summation by parts),
$\big(M_e^{-1}\Rc_{e,i}\big)_p=-\big(\nabla\cdot\FF_{h,i}-s_i\big)\big|_{\Omega_e,p}$, the strong form on $\Omega_e$'s own
interpolant. At the interior nodes of $\Omega_e$ it equals $(M^{-1}\Rc_i)_p$. At a node shared with $\Omega_{e'}$ it differs from it
by the mass-weighted jump $\jump{\nabla\cdot\FF_{h,i}}=\nabla\cdot\FF_{h,i}|_{\Omega_e}-\nabla\cdot\FF_{h,i}|_{\Omega_{e'}}$,
which the assembly averages away. On a smooth solution, therefore,
\[
R_i\big|_p=\underbrace{-\tfrac{\Delta t^2}{3}\,\partial_t^3q_{i,p}}_{\text{BDF2 truncation error, every node}}
\;+\;\underbrace{\tfrac{m_{e'}}{m_e+m_{e'}}\,\jump{\nabla\cdot\FF_{h,i}}_p}_{\text{face nodes only}} .
\]
The jump is the spatial information the paper describes: $O(h^N)$ where the solution is smooth and resolved, $O(1/h)$ across an
under-resolved discontinuity. The first term does not depend on the mesh at a given $\Delta t$: it is the floor of \S3. Computing
$\mu|_e$ at $s=1$ only keeps that floor $O(\Delta t^2)$. At a later stage the consistent three-point difference through
$(\qq^{(s)},\qq^n,\qq^{n-1})$ would carry the $O(\Delta t)$ error of the internal stage (at $N=4$, order 3.2 instead of 4.8).

\textbf{3.\ Without the cutoff: a floor that converges at order 4.}
A travelling wave has $\partial_t^3\qq=\omega^3A\neq0$ however well it is resolved, so the first term of (R) keeps $r_e$ above
\[
r_{\rm floor}=\frac{\Delta t^2}{3}\,\frac{\omega^3A}{\bar\rho\bar c}\quad(4\cdot10^{-6}\ \text{to}\ 7\cdot10^{-4}\ \text{over the sweep}),
\]
and $\mres\approx C_1\Delta_e^2\,r_{\rm floor}\propto\Delta_e^2\Delta t^2\propto h^4$ at a fixed Courant number, \emph{whatever $N$}.
The paper's asymptotic estimate keeps this $O(\Delta t^2)$ in $\Linf{R_i}{\Omega_e}$ but drops it from $\mu_{\rm res}$:
$\mu_{\rm res}=O(h^{N+2})+O(h^4)$, and the second term wins for $N>2$. That $\mu$ damps the wave by $\mu k^2t$ ($k=2\pi$), a state
error of $0.14\,\mu k^2t$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=12pt]
\item it predicts the measured error with no fitted constant ($N=6$ on $16^2$: $5.4\cdot10^{-8}$ both;
$N=5,6,7$ within $0.95$--$1.4\times$ from $8^2$ on);
\item \dsgs\ converges at $\approx4$ for $N=5,6,7$, and at equal \#DOFs the three curves coincide, since $\Delta_e$ and
$\Delta t$ are both set by the node spacing;
\item halving $\Delta t$ alone divides the error by $3.9$--$4.0$, and the Galerkin error does not move;
\item at $N=3$ the floor is ${\sim}1/100$ of Galerkin's own error, and does not show.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{4.\ With the cutoff $c=10^{-3}$.} $r_{\rm floor}<10^{-3}$ on every mesh of the sweep, so $\mu|_e\equiv0$ and \dsgs\
\emph{is} the Galerkin scheme: bit for bit, all nine variables, $L^2$ and $L^\infty$, for $N=5,6,7$ on every mesh but the
coarsest of $N=5$. The orders are Galerkin's (6.5, 6.0, 8.6 on the finest pair). Where the residual is real the cutoff barely
matters: at a discontinuity $r_e=O(10^2)$, which $c$ changes by $10^{-5}$ relative.

\textbf{5.\ What the cutoff does \emph{not} settle.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=12pt]
\item \emph{Under-resolution.} $N=5$ on $4^2$ (20 points per side, the least resolved run): $r_e>10^{-3}$ in places,
$\mu|_e$ up to $9\cdot10^{-5}$, error $3.6\times$ Galerkin's. There the viscosity acts, as it should on a marginal wave.
\item \emph{Shocks.} $c=10^{-3}$ is above this wave's floor on every mesh (at most $7\cdot10^{-4}$) and 5 decades below a
shock's $r_e$, but it has not been run on the shock cases that use (R) (Brio--Wu, jet). With (L) (Orszag--Tang, rotor, blast,
flux emergence) $r_e\approx\tfrac32|\partial_tq|/\sigma=O(1)$ wherever the flow moves, so $10^{-3}$ would change little there.
On the smooth MHD vortex ($N=6$, $32^2$, $t=0.5$) the cutoff likewise gives Galerkin bit for bit; there the excess without it
was only 2\%.
\item \emph{What $10^{-3}$ means.} $c$ is compared with $r_e$, the residual \emph{divided by} $\sigma_i$, so it is a rate in the
problem's time units: another normalization ($\erel$, element norms, \code{:dsgs\_fast\_floors}, which puts the fast speed in
place of $\bar c$) or another time scale changes the threshold it stands for.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{6.\ Where the paper's text and the code differ}, for the quantities of this note.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=12pt]
\item (11) and ``the spatial part is read from the right-hand side the solver has just assembled'' are the assembled rate of (L).
The runs with (R) (smooth vortex, Brio--Wu, jet, this wave) use the element's own $M_e^{-1}\Rc_e$ and compute $\mu|_e$ once per
step; the runs with (L) (Orszag--Tang, rotor, blast, flux emergence) evaluate (11) at every stage.
\item (16) gives $\erel=10^{-3}$. The domain-norm decks run $\erel=1$ (\code{:dsgs\_rel}); the flux-emergence decks
normalize per element instead, floored at the element's own $\rho$, $\rho c$, $\rho c^2$, $\sqrt\rho\,c$.
\item \emph{Asymptotic behavior}: $\mu_{\rm res}=O(h^{N+2})+O(\Delta_e^2\Delta t^2)$, and the second term is $O(h^4)$ at a fixed
Courant number (\S3), so without the cutoff \dsgs\ caps the order at 4.
\item \emph{Smooth vortex}: \dsgs\ on top of Galerkin up to $N=4$, diverging above as the mesh is refined, earlier for higher $N$,
is the signature of a time-difference floor of the sensor (\S3), which the paper attributes to Gibbs modes.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Reproduce.} \code{tools/alfven\_wave\_scan.sh} runs the deck as committed, (R) with $c=10^{-3}$, for $N=3\ldots7$
(\code{AW\_JOBS=5} runs the five orders at once). With \code{AW\_CUTOFF = 0.0} in \code{user\_inputs.jl},
\code{AW\_KEEP=1 AW\_VISC=dsgs tools/alfven\_wave\_scan.sh} adds the curves without the cutoff (records \code{dsgs\_nocut});
\code{JEXPRESSO\_AW\_SENSOR=legacy} selects (L). \code{dsgsCutoff\_fig.py} redraws Fig.~1 from the case's error store.
